From Spreadsheet to System: The Case for Real-Time MIS in Supply Chain Operations

When reporting lag runs 24–48 hours behind reality, you are not managing a supply chain; you are narrating one. The shift from reactive reporting to live intelligence is no longer optional.

Samriddha Basu
Operations & SCM Strategist · SIOM Nashik

The Spreadsheet Is Not the Problem. The Culture Around It Is.

Let us be clear: spreadsheets are not inherently bad. Microsoft Excel remains one of the most powerful analytical tools ever built, and Advanced Excel proficiency is genuinely valuable in operations and supply chain roles. The problem is not the tool — it is the workflow it creates when used as a primary reporting system.

The typical MIS cycle in an Indian mid-market manufacturing or distribution firm looks something like this: data is collected manually from different departments at the end of each shift or day, consolidated into a master spreadsheet by an MIS executive, formatted into a report, and shared via email to management — usually by 10 AM the following morning. By the time the report is read and acted upon, the production reality it describes is already 18–36 hours old.

A supply chain decision made on yesterday's data is not a supply chain decision. It is a historical review with operational consequences.

What I Found at JBM Group

During my engagement at JBM Group in Nashik as an MIS & Process Digitalisation Analyst, I was tasked with transforming the reporting infrastructure for production performance monitoring. What I encountered was a textbook case of the spreadsheet dependency problem.

The existing system required four separate data inputs — from quality, production, maintenance, and logistics — to be manually consolidated daily. Each department maintained its own format. Reconciliation alone consumed 2–3 hours of analyst time per day. Variance analysis happened weekly at best. When production anomalies occurred, the management team was responding to problems that had already resolved themselves, or had already cascaded into larger issues.

The intervention was not technically complex: a centralized Power BI dashboard connected to standardised input templates, with automated data validation and live variance flags. The architecture reduced reporting turnaround time by 30–40% and eliminated the need for manual reconciliation entirely. But the more significant change was cultural — when data became visible in real time, the questions people asked changed.

The Five Pillars of Effective MIS Design for SCM

1. Single Source of Truth

Every data point — production output, inventory levels, supplier delivery performance, quality rejection rates — must flow into one system. Parallel tracking in separate spreadsheets creates conflicting versions of reality, and in operations, conflicting data leads to delayed decisions and finger-pointing rather than problem-solving.

2. Input Standardisation Before Automation

The most common failure in MIS deployments is automating a broken input process. If different shifts record downtime differently, or if inventory counts use different unit definitions across warehouses, automation will amplify the inconsistency rather than resolve it. SOPs for data entry must precede the dashboard.

3. Actionable KPIs, Not Vanity Metrics

An MIS that tracks 47 metrics tracks nothing. The metrics that matter for supply chain operations are the ones that directly drive decisions: Inventory Days of Cover, Supplier On-Time Delivery Rate, Production Adherence to Schedule, and First-Pass Yield. Everything else is context, not signal.

4. Role-Based Visibility

The plant head and the shift supervisor need different views of the same data. An effective MIS gives each level of the organisation exactly the granularity they need to act — no more, no less. Dashboard overload is as dangerous as data blindness.

5. Variance Alerts, Not Just Reports

The purpose of real-time MIS is not to produce prettier reports. It is to surface anomalies before they become crises. Automated alerts triggered by threshold breaches — a production line dropping below 80% of target rate, an inventory level crossing the reorder point, a supplier delivery delayed beyond a defined window — transform MIS from a reporting tool into an early warning system.

The Oracle SCM Dimension

For organisations operating at enterprise scale, the conversation moves beyond Power BI dashboards to integrated ERP and SCM platforms. Oracle Fusion Cloud Applications — for which I hold both ERP and SCM Process Essentials certifications — provides a unified data layer across procurement, inventory, manufacturing, and logistics that eliminates the root cause of the spreadsheet problem: siloed data entry.

The difference between a well-implemented Oracle SCM environment and the spreadsheet reality described above is not just speed — it is the quality of decisions that become possible. When a procurement manager can see supplier performance alongside inventory levels alongside production schedules in a single interface, they make fundamentally better decisions about when to expedite, when to buffer, and when to renegotiate.

Where India's Supply Chains Are Heading

The pressure to digitalise supply chain reporting is no longer coming only from efficiency goals. It is coming from customers — particularly large OEMs, FMCG companies, and export-facing manufacturers — who increasingly require their suppliers to provide digital traceability, on-time delivery metrics, and quality data in standardised formats. Suppliers who cannot provide this data will find themselves displaced by those who can.

The good news is that the barrier to entry has never been lower. Power BI is inexpensive. Oracle Cloud applications are available on subscription models. Python for data pipeline automation is free. The investment required is not primarily financial — it is the willingness to standardise processes, train people, and commit to a single version of operational truth.

The supply chains that will define Indian manufacturing competitiveness over the next decade are not being built around better machines. They are being built around better information — faster, more accurate, and more actionable than anything a spreadsheet can provide.

A Practical First Step

If you are running a supply chain operation and wondering where to begin, start with your biggest information bottleneck — the report that is always late, always disputed, and always used to justify inaction rather than drive it. Standardise the inputs. Build a live dashboard. Measure the time-to-decision improvement over 90 days. That number will make your next digitalisation investment decision very straightforward.
